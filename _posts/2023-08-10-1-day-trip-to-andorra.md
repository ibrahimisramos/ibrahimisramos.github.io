<p>After hearing how near Andorra is from my friends (Daryus – my roommate) who came in period 1 of my summer school, I knew I wanted to visit a new country especially because of its proximity. Besides, increasing country count is a crazy obsession I do have (it’s a healthier obsession than body count and I’m not hurting anyone just my wallet so all is good). My goal is to visit the same number of countries as my age when I hit 50. Anyways, coming back, my friends from SUSS, Jerrell and Renz were saying how they plan to go to these 3 countries in this 1-day tour over the weekend and since I had no weekend plans and I felt FOMO (Fear of Missing out), me and Valent succumbed to signing up for the <a href="https://www.tripadvisor.com/AttractionProductReview-g187497-d11448538-The_Original_Three_Countries_in_One_Day_France_Andorra_Spain_from_Barcelona-Barcel.html">tour</a> to Andorra and France. </p>
<p>Little did I know that my day would start with a small hiccup (my dear friends would argue it was a major one) as I missed the train to the city center. We wanted to meet at 5.30am at their villa so that we have ample time to reach the city center at 7am (takes about 45 mins + factoring in delays). I arrived late (apologies for my Indian timing) because I wanted to eat breakfast before coming since I had cereal to finish and I figured, what could possibly go wrong with being a few minutes late? I mean we have plenty of time to spare for me to relax and eat my cereal with milk. I was proven wrong almost immediately as my friends told me that the train was at 5.42am and the next train was 35 minutes later (6.17am) so I needed to hurry up. I gobbled down my breakfast and in a state of half-consciousness and a heavy stomach, tried to walk fast. At this point, it was 5.36am and I had 6 minutes to cover a distance that normally takes 12-15 minutes to walk. I dragged myself, running as fast as my body could carry me. At 5.38am, I could hear the train moving and I remember wishing sincerely that I could reach in time. After much struggle, I did reach the train station at 5.41am but as soon as I took the lift up, the train had just departed and my friends had already left. I was dismayed for 2 very simple reasons – Why did the train have to leave early like the one time (being very optimistic here) I’m late and if I wait for the next train, will I make it in time. I was panicking profusely throughout, dutifully self-blaming myself for being late and being a burden (not healthy btw) till some meditation and reiki helped me calm down. Taking the taxi is too expensive (60 Euros) and not really worth it and also I needed to install an app to call it which I have no idea about. I eventually tried to see things more positively and convinced myself that it was good to get some alone time and that my friends could help me stall time till I came. </p>
<p>I reached Plaza Catalunya (the main city center) at 6.56am and tried to rush to the place but only reached around 7.08 because quite a few roadworks were undergoing and there was a glitch in the live location Jerrell sent. Also, at that point, another tour guide from the same company convinced me to leave my friends and join his group so I got delayed. Nonetheless, I boarded the bus around 7.10 and we immediately set off. Thanks Jerrell, Valent and Renz for stalling for me and sorry for the mental torture I put you guys through. </p>

<p><strong>Andorra </strong></p>
<p>First things first, the national language of Andorra is Catalan. Wow, who would have thought. So Andorra is not in the EU (so no data sadly from our plan we got in Spain) and is the 6th smallest city in Europe, after Vatican City, San Marino, Monaco, Liechtenstein and Malta. Another interesting thing is that Andorra is the only place under 2 different jurisdictions – Spain and France. They did make their own government in the 1980s which is co-headed by the bishop of Urgell in Catalonia, Spain and the president of France, Macron. Andorra, which uses Euro, has a total population of 70000 but only 1/3 are native Andorrans. This is because to be considered a resident, you need to live there for 6 months a year and own a house there (which costs around 50,000 euros) and people move here as Andorra is known to be a tax haven with just 10% tax rates. People also use this country to hide dirty money. Nevertheless, its a famous tourist spot with about 10 million visitors yearly and the easiest way up there is from Barcelona as there is no airport here (how did I not know this before I came here?). They joined the UN in 1993 and their flag has 3 colours and is essentially a combination of France and Spain. They have the highest capital (because they are on a mountain) in Europe. Education is free here and ski lessons are included as part of their curriculum. Sadly, they have never won any Olympic medals in their history. So we had to go through a “customs” for immigration as more of a formality. Because we were on a tour group, our tour guide helped us get an Andorra stamp.</p>
