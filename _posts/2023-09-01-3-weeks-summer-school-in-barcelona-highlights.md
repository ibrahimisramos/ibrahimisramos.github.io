<p>So in a rather last-minute attempt to travel the world, I was looking at summer school ideas and found a related module I could take to satisfy my requirement. Besides, I always wanted to go to Spain ever since my time in Germany. Also, it allows me to reach my goal of 50 countries by the age of 50 years old.</p>

<li>1<sup>st</sup> day of school + Sushi buffet + Grocery shopping</li>
</ol>
<p>I was shocked that on my first day of school (the 2<sup>nd</sup> day of my arrival), almost 20 Singaporeans were gathering to go to class together. Turns out Daryus met many of them the day before when they went Monserrat (which I bailed because I was tired after the flight) and they wanted to go together for the opening briefing.</p>
<figure><img alt="" loading="lazy" src="/assets/img/travel/3-weeks-summer-school-in-barcelona-highlights/welcome-meeting.jpg"/></figure>
<p>I met 2 Singaporeans from NUS nursing and I asked them why they came to Barcelona and they were recommended by their professors to avoid other popular destinations that Singaporeans flock to like Netherlands to have more interactions with other international students but I told them there were over 20 Singaporeans here. Sadly, I never saw them ever again. Nonetheless, nice campus and we were a 10-minute walk to class and with many Singaporeans, what could I ask for? Could never feel homesick here.</p>

<li>Data Protection class + Friends made</li>
</ol>
<p>Main reason I came to UAB (Universiti Autonoma Barcelona was to study this module titled Data Protection Legislation and Ethics (which NUS Computing allowed me to map to IS4233, a core module of mine). I was really excited by the prospects of learning about GDPR from the experts itself and the content of AI ethics and Privacy Enhancing Technologies (PET) was in line with the course CIPT (Certified Information Privacy Technologist) I am studying for. I also got to learn about the new AI act that the EU is going to pass soon</p>
<p>The first few days were a bit dry with legal stuff and everyone already had a friend group except me. Thankfully, I met Carlos on the 4<sup>th</sup> day of class as he had to miss the 1<sup>st</sup> 3 days of class due to a prior engagement. I initially talked to him because he was sitting nearby me and I thought we could talk since he was Chinese-looking and wanted to impress him with some limited Chinese. Turns out he was Chinese-Cantonese but Spanish and he gave me a biscuit and broke the ice very well. He was really nice and by the 2<sup>nd</sup> day he walked me to the Mosque and recommended me good places.</p>

<p>Daryus one day just told me he was going to rent a car to go to Costa Brava (outside of Barcelona) to Kayak with 4 other people and I was like, I want to come too. Then we were discussing because the car will be a bit full so perhaps we should open up to more people. However, we needed to have another driver because Vikrant did not want to drive. Also, another issue was that no one had an international license and if we do get stopped it’s&gt; 100 euros fine. Surprisingly to everyone, I had an international driving license as I got it to drive the year before in Turkey with my family but did not manage to.</p>
<p>The plan became for 12 people to go in two cars, with Vikrant guiding me as it was my first time driving on the left and I was scared. We took a car each (which cost about 90 euros for a day, which IMO is a bit expensive) and rushed to the airport (which is the only place to rent a car). On the way we stopped to get lunch at this Kebab place and found Lemacun (Turkish pizza wrap) and it was just amazing.</p>

<ol start="5">
<li>Driving manual + Kayaking at Costa Brava</li>
</ol>
<p>So now, after our last week’s fiasco, we became smarter and changed our plan. We intended to rent the car late at night (take the last Aerobus from Plaza Catalunya to the Airport) about 11pm and after going to Costa Brava, drop everyone back and return the car. This time, however, I was on my own as Vikrant was not coming. We had 11 people going and me and Daryus were the drivers.</p>
<p> So we went to get the car and when we were in the airport, we were told that there were no auto cars because some people did not return the car. Thus, we were basically given 2 options. 1 – Come back the next day and by then hopefully they will have auto cars for us. 2 – We can take our pick from their display of many manual cars, drive it for now and come back the next morning to exchange for a manual car. We were thinking of what to do because it cost us 11.75 euros to take the Aerobus, also Daryus only has an auto licence and he didn’t know I had a manual license.</p>
<p>After a while, I mustered the courage to voice out that I had a manual license but it had been 4 years since I got it and never driven a manual car since. I requested the car dealer if he give me a refresher course and sit in the car with me till I got it right.</p>
<p>He happily obliged and I rode the car a few rounds around the carpark till I got the hang of things. I was nervous throughout and thankfully his encouraging words, of how he has seen worse people who claim they can drive manual cars but cant, reassured me and I drove Daryus back to our accommodation in Campus. Thanks Daryus for trusting me enough with your life and staying in the car throughout my practice period. Means a lot.</p>
