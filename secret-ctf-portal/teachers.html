Before the session

  • Any laptop with Chrome, Edge or Firefox works. School-managed Chromebooks often block Developer Tools (F12), and that policy usually blocks view-source: too, so test one device first. On a locked-down device, challenges 5, 6, 7 and 8 still work (address bar, CyberChef and reading the site). Challenges 1 and 2 need view-source, and 3 and 4 need DevTools.
  • Students need the portal link (or let them hunt for it from the home page: challenge zero is finding it through robots.txt).
  • Progress is saved per browser, so students should use the same device throughout.

Suggested 60-minute flow

  1. 10 min: What is a hacker? Ethical hacking vs crime, and why permission matters.
  2. 5 min: Demo challenge 1 together on the projector.
  3. 35 min: Students work in pairs. Encourage hints before asking for help.
  4. 10 min: Debrief: which challenge surprised them, and the "real jobs" sections.

An important message for students

These skills are only OK to use on systems you have permission to test, like this one. Trying them on other websites without permission is illegal in Singapore under the Computer Misuse Act. If they enjoyed this, point them to legal practice platforms such as picoCTF, which was built for school students.
